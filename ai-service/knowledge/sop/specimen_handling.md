# SOP: Specimen Handling, Transport and Storage

## Purpose

To preserve specimen integrity between collection and analysis so that results
reflect the patient's actual state rather than degradation in transit.

## Scope

Applies to collection centres, courier staff and central laboratory receiving.

## Handling immediately after collection

- Keep tubes upright in a rack. Upright storage reduces haemolysis and speeds
  clot formation in serum tubes.
- Allow serum tubes to clot fully before centrifugation — normally 30 minutes
  at room temperature.
- Protect light-sensitive analytes, such as bilirubin, from direct light.
- Do not shake tubes. Vigorous agitation ruptures red cells and haemolyses the
  sample.

## Centrifugation

Where the laboratory requires separation before transport, centrifuge at the
speed and duration specified for the tube type, then transport the separated
sample. Delayed separation is a common cause of falsely raised potassium and
falsely lowered glucose.

## Transport temperature categories

| Category | Condition | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient | 15–25 °C | Most routine chemistry and haematology |
| Refrigerated | 2–8 °C | Samples with delayed transport, some hormones |
| Frozen | −20 °C or below | Selected send-away and specialised assays |

Each category has a defined maximum transit window. A sample that exceeds its
window or arrives outside its temperature range is flagged at receiving and
assessed before analysis.

## Courier runs

Branch samples move to the central laboratory on scheduled courier runs.
Urgent-priority samples are placed in the marked urgent carrier and trigger an
unscheduled run when the next scheduled run would exceed the urgent turnaround
commitment.

## Receiving at the central laboratory

1. Confirm the sample count against the manifest.
2. Check labels, tube types, volumes and transport condition.
3. Record the receipt timestamp against the accession number — this timestamp
   starts the laboratory's turnaround clock.
4. Route accepted samples to the correct workstation queue.
5. Record rejections with reason and notify the originating branch.

## Storage after analysis

Analysed samples are retained under the storage condition and retention period
defined for that specimen type so that add-on tests and repeat testing remain
possible. Disposal follows the laboratory's clinical waste procedure.
