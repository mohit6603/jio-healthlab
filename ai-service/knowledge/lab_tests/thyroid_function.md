# Thyroid Function Tests

## What a thyroid function test measures

A thyroid function test (TFT) assesses how well the thyroid gland is working by
measuring the hormones that control and are produced by it. The thyroid sits at
the front of the neck and regulates metabolic rate, body temperature, heart
rate and energy use.

## Components of the panel

- **TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone)** — released by the pituitary gland to
  tell the thyroid how much hormone to produce. TSH is usually the most
  sensitive first-line marker, because the pituitary responds to small changes
  in thyroid hormone before the hormones themselves move out of range.
- **Free T4 (thyroxine)** — the main hormone the thyroid secretes. "Free"
  refers to the unbound fraction that is biologically active.
- **Free T3 (triiodothyronine)** — the more metabolically active hormone, much
  of it converted from T4 in peripheral tissues.
- **Thyroid antibodies** (anti-TPO, anti-thyroglobulin) — added when an
  autoimmune cause is being investigated.

## The feedback relationship

TSH and thyroid hormones move in opposite directions in most situations. When
thyroid hormone output falls, the pituitary raises TSH to compensate; when
output rises, TSH is suppressed. This inverse relationship is why TSH and free
T4 are usually interpreted together rather than separately.

## Why a clinician orders a TFT

Common reasons include investigating fatigue, unexplained weight change, cold
or heat intolerance, hair or skin changes, palpitations, menstrual changes, and
monitoring patients already on thyroid replacement or antithyroid treatment.

## Understanding the terminology

- **Hypothyroidism** — an underactive thyroid; the gland produces less hormone
  than the body needs.
- **Hyperthyroidism** — an overactive thyroid.
- **Subclinical** — a pattern where TSH is outside the range but the free
  hormones are still within it.
- **Euthyroid** — thyroid function within the expected range.

## Preparation and sample requirements

Fasting is not generally required. Timing can matter: TSH follows a daily
rhythm and tends to be higher in the early morning, so serial monitoring is
best done at a consistent time of day. Biotin supplements can interfere with
some thyroid immunoassays and should be declared before the sample is taken.

The sample is serum, usually collected into a plain or gel separator tube.

## Turnaround

Thyroid assays are run in batches on immunoassay analysers. Routine panels are
normally resulted within one working day; urgent requests are prioritised.
