# Liver Function Tests

## What a liver function test measures

A liver function test (LFT), also called a hepatic panel, measures enzymes and
proteins that reflect how the liver is working and whether liver cells or the
bile drainage system are under stress. Despite the name, several components are
markers of liver *injury* rather than of liver *function*.

## Components of the panel

- **ALT (alanine aminotransferase)** — an enzyme concentrated in liver cells.
  It is released into blood when those cells are damaged, making it a
  relatively liver-specific injury marker.
- **AST (aspartate aminotransferase)** — a similar enzyme also found in heart
  and skeletal muscle, so it is less liver-specific than ALT.
- **ALP (alkaline phosphatase)** — associated with the bile ducts; also present
  in bone, so it rises during periods of bone growth.
- **GGT (gamma-glutamyl transferase)** — helps establish whether a raised ALP
  is coming from the liver or from bone.
- **Bilirubin (total and direct)** — a pigment produced when red blood cells
  break down, processed by the liver and excreted in bile.
- **Albumin** — the main protein made by the liver; it reflects synthetic
  capacity over weeks rather than days.
- **Total protein** — albumin plus globulins.

## Injury markers versus function markers

ALT, AST, ALP and GGT indicate that liver cells or bile ducts are irritated.
Albumin, bilirubin and clotting studies speak to what the liver is still able
to *do*. A panel can show markedly raised enzymes with preserved synthetic
function, or the reverse — which is why the pattern across the whole panel
matters more than any single value.

## Why a clinician orders an LFT

Reasons include investigating jaundice, abdominal pain, unexplained fatigue or
nausea; monitoring patients on medicines that can affect the liver; screening
in the context of alcohol use; and following up an abnormal previous result.

## Understanding the terminology

- **Jaundice** — yellowing of skin and eyes caused by raised bilirubin.
- **Cholestasis** — impaired bile flow, typically raising ALP and GGT.
- **Hepatocellular** pattern — enzyme rise dominated by ALT and AST.
- **Synthetic function** — the liver's ability to manufacture proteins such as
  albumin and clotting factors.

## Preparation and sample requirements

Fasting is not usually required for an LFT alone, but is often requested when
it is ordered alongside a lipid profile or glucose test. Recent alcohol intake
and some medicines and supplements can alter results and should be declared.

The sample is serum or lithium-heparin plasma. Haemolysed samples — where red
cells have ruptured — are rejected for several analytes because the released
cell contents distort the measurement.
