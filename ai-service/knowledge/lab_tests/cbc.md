# Complete Blood Count (CBC)

## What a CBC measures

A complete blood count is a routine screening panel that measures the cells
circulating in a blood sample. It reports three broad cell populations:

- **Red blood cells (RBC)** — the cells that carry oxygen from the lungs to the
  rest of the body.
- **White blood cells (WBC)** — the cells of the immune system that respond to
  infection and inflammation.
- **Platelets** — small cell fragments that help blood clot.

Because it covers all three populations in a single sample, the CBC is one of
the most frequently ordered tests in any laboratory.

## Components of the panel

| Component | What it describes |
|---|---|
| Haemoglobin (Hb) | The oxygen-carrying protein inside red blood cells |
| Haematocrit (Hct) | The proportion of blood volume made up of red cells |
| RBC count | The number of red blood cells per unit volume |
| MCV | Mean corpuscular volume — the average size of a red cell |
| MCH / MCHC | The average amount and concentration of haemoglobin per cell |
| RDW | Red cell distribution width — how variable red cell size is |
| WBC count | The total number of white blood cells |
| Differential | The proportion of neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils and basophils |
| Platelet count | The number of platelets per unit volume |
| MPV | Mean platelet volume — the average platelet size |

## Why a clinician orders a CBC

A CBC is ordered as part of a general health check, before surgery, to
investigate symptoms such as fatigue, bruising, fever or unexplained weight
loss, and to monitor patients on treatments that affect the bone marrow. It is
a broad screening tool: it points towards areas that may need further
investigation rather than confirming a specific condition on its own.

## Understanding the terminology

- **Anaemia** describes a haemoglobin level below the expected range for a
  patient's age and sex. It is a finding, not a diagnosis — the underlying
  cause still has to be established.
- **Leukocytosis** means a raised white cell count; **leukopenia** means a
  reduced one.
- **Thrombocytopenia** means a reduced platelet count; **thrombocytosis** means
  a raised one.
- A **differential** breaks the white cell count into its subtypes, which helps
  distinguish between different kinds of immune response.

## Sample requirements

A CBC is performed on whole blood collected into an EDTA tube (commonly a
lavender-topped tube). The tube must be filled to the indicated volume and
gently inverted several times immediately after collection so the
anticoagulant mixes evenly. Under-filled or clotted samples are rejected
because the cell counts they produce are unreliable.

Fasting is not required for a CBC.

## Turnaround

A routine CBC is typically resulted within a few hours of the sample reaching
the analyser. Samples flagged urgent are prioritised in the processing queue.

## Reference ranges

Reference ranges vary by laboratory, analyser, age, sex, altitude and
pregnancy status. Always read a result against the range printed on the report
that produced it rather than against a range from a different laboratory.
