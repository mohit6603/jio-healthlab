# Diagnostic Imaging

## What imaging shows

Imaging produces pictures of structures inside the body. Unlike a blood or
urine test, which measures substances in a sample, imaging is looked at and
described by a reporting clinician — which is why its turnaround is longer.

## X-Ray

An X-ray passes a small amount of ionising radiation through the body onto a
detector. Dense tissue such as bone absorbs more and appears lighter; air
absorbs least and appears darkest.

X-rays are quick, widely available and inexpensive, and are well suited to
bone and chest imaging. They show soft tissue poorly.

**Preparation.** Usually none. Metal objects in the imaged area are removed,
as they obscure what is behind them. Anyone who is or may be pregnant should
say so before the examination, because the imaging plan may change.

**Duration.** Typically a few minutes.

## MRI Scan

Magnetic resonance imaging uses a strong magnetic field and radio waves rather
than ionising radiation. It distinguishes soft tissue far better than an X-ray,
which makes it the tool of choice for the brain, spinal cord, joints and many
internal organs.

**Preparation.** Metal is the central concern. The magnetic field is always on,
so implanted devices, surgical clips, and metal fragments must be declared
before entry to the scanner room. Some examinations require fasting, and some
use a contrast agent.

**Duration.** Typically 20 to 60 minutes depending on the region. The scanner
is loud, and the patient must stay still — movement blurs the images and can
require a repeat.

## X-ray or MRI

| | X-Ray | MRI |
|---|---|---|
| Radiation | Ionising, low dose | None |
| Best for | Bone, chest | Soft tissue, brain, joints |
| Duration | Minutes | 20–60 minutes |
| Metal implants | Usually acceptable | Must be declared; may be a contraindication |
| Availability | Widely available | Scheduled, limited slots |

The choice belongs to the requesting clinician and depends on what needs to be
seen.

## Why imaging turnaround is longer

An imaging study is reported, not measured. The images are acquired, checked
for quality, then read and described by a reporting clinician. That reporting
step is scheduled work, which is why the laboratory's targets for imaging are
longer than for a blood panel — 12 hours routine for an X-ray and 48 hours for
an MRI, against 4 hours for a complete blood count.

## Understanding the terminology

- **Contrast agent** — a substance given to make particular structures stand
  out on the images.
- **Radiolucent / radiopaque** — appearing dark or light on an X-ray according
  to how much radiation the tissue absorbs.
- **Sequence** — one of several MRI acquisition settings, each showing tissue
  differently.
- **Artefact** — an appearance on the image caused by the equipment, movement
  or metal rather than by anatomy.
